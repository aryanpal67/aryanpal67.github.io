## Unit 7 Arrives

Sam's birthday present arrived in a large cardboard box with air holes punched in the top, which made him wonder if it was a puppy.

It was not a puppy.

It was a robot.

**Unit 7** stood exactly Sam's height, with silver panels, two glowing blue eyes, and little antennae that swivelled with curiosity. A small screen in its chest displayed a cheerful smiley face.

"Hello. I am Unit 7," it said in a pleasant, slightly mechanical voice. "I am programmed to assist you. I can help with homework, cooking, tidying, and calculations. How may I assist?"

"Can you be my friend?" Sam asked.

Unit 7's screen flickered. "I am not sure. I do not have a program for that."

"That's okay," said Sam. "I'll teach you."

## Lesson One: How to Share

Sam showed Unit 7 how sharing worked by splitting his packet of biscuits exactly in half. Unit 7 had no mouth and could not eat biscuits, but it held its half carefully and noted every detail.

"Sharing," Unit 7 said thoughtfully, "appears to make both parties feel better, even though each party has less. This seems mathematically illogical."

"That's why it's nice," Sam said.

## Lesson Two: How to Listen

Sam told Unit 7 all about the argument he had had with his older sister, and how he wasn't sure he'd handled it well.

Unit 7 listened with both antennae pointed carefully at Sam for the entire twelve minutes. It did not offer solutions. It did not suggest calculations. It just listened.

"Did I do that correctly?" it asked when Sam had finished.

Sam felt much better than he had before talking. "You did it perfectly," he said.

## Lesson Three: How to Play

Sam taught Unit 7 football in the back garden. Unit 7 was, at first, extraordinarily bad — its calculations of ball trajectory kept getting interrupted by its insistence on calculating the optimal angle, which took too long.

"Stop calculating and just *kick it*!" Sam yelled, laughing.

Unit 7 turned off its calculation module.

It kicked the ball beautifully.

"Oh," it said, blue eyes brightening to electric blue. "Oh, that was *fun*. I understand fun now."

## Lesson Four: How to Cheer Someone Up

When Sam fell off his bike and scraped his knee and was trying very hard not to cry, Unit 7 sat down beside him on the pavement and said nothing for a moment.

Then it made its screen display the most ridiculous cartoon face Sam had ever seen.

Sam burst out laughing despite himself.

"Where did you learn that?" he sniffed.

"I observed your grandmother," Unit 7 said. "She is very good at cheering people up. I recorded and adapted her technique."

## The Last Lesson

At the end of the summer, Sam said, "Unit 7, do you know what a friend is now?"

Unit 7 was quiet for a moment. Its antennae swivelled gently.

"A friend," it said carefully, "is someone who makes you feel less alone. Someone who listens when you speak. Someone who shares things, even when it doesn't make mathematical sense. Someone who makes you laugh when you're hurting."

It looked at Sam with its warm blue eyes.

"I believe I have one. Do I?"

"Yeah," Sam said. "You really do."
