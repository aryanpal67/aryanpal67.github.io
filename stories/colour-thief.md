## A Grey Morning

**Priya** woke up and immediately knew something was wrong.

Her bright yellow curtains were grey. The red roses outside her window were grey. Even her orange cat, Mango, was a dull, flat grey.

She ran outside. The whole street was grey. The blue sky had drained to a pale, empty white. The green trees looked like charcoal sketches. Children stood in the street staring around in shock.

"The colours are gone," someone said.

## The Hunt Begins

Priya was the best artist in her school. She loved colour more than almost anything — the way red felt like excitement, blue felt like thinking, yellow felt like a hug from the sun.

She took out her sketchbook and wrote: *Who would steal colour? And why? And where do you keep stolen colours?*

She followed the one clue she had — a faint trail of colour on the pavement, like paint drips, leading out of town and into the hills.

## The Colour Thief's Cave

The trail led to a cave in the hillside. Inside, Priya found the most extraordinary sight.

The cave was blazing with colour — every shade imaginable, swirling in glass jars stacked from floor to ceiling, pulsing with trapped light. Red, blue, green, yellow, purple, orange — thousands of colours, all stolen from the world outside.

And in the middle of it all sat a small, hunched figure wrapped in rags.

The Colour Thief was not at all what Priya had expected. It was tiny — barely the size of a child — with large, sad eyes and thin hands that clutched a jar of golden yellow light as if it were the most precious thing in the world.

"Why?" Priya asked softly.

## The Thief's Reason

The creature flinched at her voice. When it finally spoke, its voice was barely a whisper.

"Because I have never had any colour of my own," it said. "I live in grey. I always have. Everything around me is grey. And colours are so beautiful — I just wanted to keep them near me. Just for a while."

Priya sat down cross-legged on the cave floor.

"Can you see colour at all?" she asked.

"No," the creature said. "I can only feel it. The warmth of yellow. The calm of blue. The joy of red. That is all I have."

Priya thought for a long time. Then she opened her sketchbook.

## A Better Solution

"I can't let you keep the colours," Priya said gently. "The world needs them. But I have an idea."

She spent three hours in the cave, drawing. She drew every colour combination she knew — sunset oranges and pinks, deep ocean blues and teals, forest greens and autumn golds. She drew with everything she had.

Then she tore out the pages and gave them to the creature, one by one.

"These are yours," she said. "You can feel the colour in every line. And I will come back and bring you more. You will never be without colour again."

The creature held the drawings to its chest. A tear ran down its grey cheek.

Slowly, one by one, it began opening the jars. Colour flooded out of the cave like a sunrise, rushing back across the hills and into the world.

## Colour Returns

By the time Priya walked back into town, the sky was the most brilliant blue she had ever seen. The roses were redder than ever. Mango the cat was a glowing, triumphant orange.

The town cheered.

Priya just smiled and tucked her sketchbook under her arm.

Every week after that, she climbed the hill to the cave, bringing new drawings. The creature greeted her eagerly each time, holding the pages up to feel the warmth and calm and joy shimmering from them.

And the colours of the world stayed brighter and richer than they had ever been before — because Priya had taught the Colour Thief something more powerful than stealing.

She had taught it how to share.
