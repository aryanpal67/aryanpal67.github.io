## The Sky at Night

Up in the velvet darkness of the night sky, billions of stars blazed and twinkled — all except one.

Right in the corner of the sky, between the Big Dipper and the Pleiades, a very small star named **Pip** sat with its light turned all the way down.

"What if I shine too brightly and people stare?" Pip worried. "What if I'm not as beautiful as the other stars? What if nobody even notices me?"

The big stars tried to help. **Orion** said, "Every star has its own light, little one." **Polaris** said, "The sky needs all of us to be complete." But Pip just dimmed a little more and hugged its tiny glow to itself.

## The Little Girl

Late one autumn night, a girl named **Leila** was walking home from her grandmother's house through the woods. She had walked this path a hundred times — but tonight, clouds had swallowed every star, and she had taken a wrong turn in the darkness.

She sat down at the foot of an oak tree, frightened and cold.

"I just need one light," she said quietly. "Just one, to show me the way."

## Pip's Decision

In the sky above, Pip heard her.

For a long moment, Pip hesitated, all the old fears crowding in. *What if I'm not bright enough? What if it doesn't work? What if—*

Then Pip looked down at the small, frightened girl huddled beneath the tree.

*She just needs one light. And I am a light.*

Slowly, shakily — Pip began to shine.

## The Glow

It started small — just a faint golden glow between the clouds. But Pip kept going, pushing past the fear, and the glow grew warmer and brighter and steadier.

Leila looked up and gasped.

One perfect star had appeared in the gap between the clouds, casting a slender golden path of light down through the trees — pointing directly at the right fork in the path.

She followed it all the way home.

## The Sky Afterwards

After that night, Pip never dimmed again.

Not because everyone stared (though some did), not because the other stars approved (though they did), but because Pip had learned something that no star-chart could teach:

*Your light is not for you. It is for everyone who needs to find their way in the dark.*

And of all the stars in the night sky, if you know where to look, Pip is the one that shines the steadiest — warm and golden and utterly, joyfully, unafraid.
