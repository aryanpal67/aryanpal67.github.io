## The Shadow on the Mountain

Every morning, the village of Sundale woke to a shadow on the mountain — a vast, dark shape that circled slowly above the highest peak, where clouds gathered year-round.

"The dragon," the villagers would whisper, closing their shutters.

No one had been brave enough to climb Cloud Mountain in living memory. No one except **a boy named Aryan**.

## Up the Mountain

Aryan had watched the dragon every day from his window. But unlike the other villagers, he didn't see danger in those circling wings. He saw something much sadder.

*Loneliness.*

One misty morning, while everyone else was still asleep, Aryan packed a bag with:
- 🍞 Fresh bread from the bakery
- 🍎 Six shiny apples
- 📚 His favourite book of adventure stories
- 🎵 His little wooden flute

He climbed all morning through pine forests and rocky paths, until he finally emerged above the clouds.

## Meeting Ember

The dragon was enormous — scales like burnished copper and gold, wings wide as a house, eyes the colour of autumn leaves. When it spotted Aryan, it reared back and roared a great plume of orange flame.

Aryan stood very still.

"Hello," he said. "My name is Aryan. I brought you apples. Do you like apples?"

The dragon blinked. It looked at the small boy. It looked at the apples. It lowered its enormous head.

Very carefully, very gently, it took an apple from Aryan's outstretched hand.

"I thought so," Aryan smiled. "What's your name?"

The dragon puffed a small smoke ring.

"Ember," it said, in a voice like crackling fire. "Nobody has spoken to me in three hundred years."

## The Dragon's Story

Ember had not always lived alone on the mountain. Long ago, the village had been her home too. She had helped farmers light their hearths in winter, guided sailors home through the dark with her glowing wings, and kept the village safe from wolves.

But one frightened night, many generations ago, a new family had moved to Sundale. They had never seen a dragon before. They screamed. Others panicked. And Ember — confused and heartbroken — had retreated to the mountain.

"I never meant to frighten anyone," Ember said quietly. A small flame flickered from her nostril. "I was only trying to be helpful."

Aryan sat down beside her enormous clawed foot. "That's the saddest story I've ever heard."

## A New Beginning

Aryan read to Ember from his adventure book. He played his flute, and Ember hummed along in her deep, rumbling voice. They watched the sunset paint the clouds gold and pink.

The next morning, Aryan went back to the village and told everyone Ember's story. At first they were afraid. But one brave grandmother said, "If an eight-year-old can sit with a dragon, so can I."

One by one, the villagers climbed the mountain to meet Ember.

By the end of the week, Ember was helping light the bakery ovens, warming the schoolhouse on cold mornings, and giving the children rides on her magnificent back.

Sundale became the most famous village in the kingdom — not for its fear of a dragon, but for its friendship with one.

And every night, Ember slept curled around the mountain, her warm glow visible for miles — no longer a shadow, but a beacon.
